## M12 "behind": judgement

The leak claim is correct. Sec. 4 and Sec. 5 together show that the CNN was trained with phoneme labels on 5/6 of the shuffled frames of all 300 words, including the 84 test words. Test speakers are seen, and the authors say so ("closed-speaker"). The rule of three is met by read priors that keep every trained component away from test utterances. Duchnowski (one speaker, supervised LDA fitted on training data only) and Ngiam (explicitly no test data in feature learning) are the strongest and most comparable.

The verdict holds against best comparable work. It should rest on the frame leak, not on seen speakers. Cox states that the seen-speaker mode was widely adopted, and only 36 of 88 priors mention speaker independence. The one-line verdict ("speakers are seen (M12 behind)") and the M12 table cell merge the two reasons.

## M06 "standard": judgement

The label is correct under the verdict rules, because the gain over PCA is not repeated. A second reason is missing. The CNN is the only feature trained, with labels, on the test-word frames; PCA and 6×6 are unsupervised. The M12 leak therefore inflates the M06 comparison itself. The strongest confound is supervision combined with exposure to the test words, not supervision alone.

## Required fixes, by impact

1. **High.** Tie the leak to the central M06 claim. In the Verdict and in "Where the claims outrun the evidence", state that only the CNN was fitted, with labels, on frames of the 84 test words. Its gain over PCA and 6×6 (Fig. 5) is therefore confounded by test exposure as well as by supervision. Make this the strongest confound.
2. **High.** Fix the missing-control citation. A labelled network on PCA inputs is Duchnowski [2] (32 PCs vs raw pixels, Table 1: 52/45 vs 55/44), not Heckmann [3], whose inputs are chroma-key lip parameters. Say that this control existed in 1994 and that the target did not run it.
3. **Medium.** Correct the M06 usual-approach count in the table, slots.json (`matched`) and the Appendix. "HMM over hand-crafted features" is not 79. Either relabel the design as "HMM classifier" (79 of 88) or recount the conjunction (61 of 88 by my recount; inference). Likewise for M12, `matched: 36` counts speaker-independent mentions, not "held-out utterances". Relabel it, or set `matched` to null.
4. **Medium.** M12 comparators: name Duchnowski as the best comparable prior (single speaker, like the target). Quote its "computed using only the training set" and Ngiam's "no test data was used for unsupervised feature learning". Drop "seen speakers" as a reason for behind, or label it typical of the field per Cox Sec. 3. Separate the two in verdict.json `short`.
5. **Medium.** Validate the M12 absence count. Normalise ligatures (ﬁ, ﬂ, ﬀ) in the text before matching. Widen random_frame_split so that it matches the target's own sentence and [7], then restate "0 of 88" and the variability count (22 after normalisation, by my recount).
6. **Medium.** Narrow the novelty sentence to "spatial (2-D) convolution over mouth images". Duchnowski's MS-TDNN already applies shared-weight temporal convolution to lip pixels.
7. **Low–medium.** Fix the internal contradiction "84 unseen words" (M06 SOTA line) to "84 test words unseen by the HMM". Reword the first question for the authors. Sec. 4 already answers it ("all 300 words were used"), while the Verdict asserts the leak. Ask instead how word accuracy changes when the CNN is retrained without frames of the test words.
8. **Low–medium.** Name the missing priors: Lan et al. 2010 (cited by the target, 12-fold speaker-independent CV); the same group's Yoshida et al. AVSP 2010 on the same corpus line; Huang & Kingsbury ICASSP 2013 (DBN AVSR, outside the ledger, cited by [7]). Describe them through their citations and label them.
9. **Low.** M07: monophone-HMM word recognition implies a pronunciation lexicon of the 84 test words, so "no lexicon reported" is inaccurate. Write "closed 84-word pronunciation lexicon, no LM", and state chance (about 1.2%) beside the 37%.
10. **Low.** Credit the authors' own statements: the "closed-speaker" label (Sec. 6.3); the disclosure that all 300 words trained the CNN (Sec. 4); their speaker-independent future work (Sec. 7), which overlaps "What the field needs next" M12; and their label-procedure recommendation (Sec. 6.1), which overlaps the M02 item. Add the Sec. 7 concession quote. Replace "meeting the rule of three" with plain wording, since it reads as process language.
11. **Low.** Bibliography: add the ACM DOI 10.5555/3104482.3104569 and pages for Ngiam (now URL only). Write Petridis as "2020 (arXiv 2019)" consistently. Mention that the phoneme-level result (Fig. 2) is a mean and SD over six speakers, so "without seeds" is not read as "no repetition at all".

## Scores

- Procedure: 7/10. Population, dating and tallies reproduce exactly, priors were read, and the leak was found. Against that, a conjunction count is mislabelled, the absence regex fails on the target itself because of ligatures, and recall gaps drop the target's own cited prior and same-group priors.
- Final text: 7/10. Clear position, correct core facts and properly labelled [concurrent] work. Against that, it cites the wrong source for the key control, contradicts itself on "unseen words", does not connect the leak to the central comparison, and mixes the seen-speaker point into the behind verdict.

The review's strongest finding, the test-word leak into CNN training, is correct and well supported. Its use of the authors' own journal version as a tacit fix, with care not to overread it, is sound.
