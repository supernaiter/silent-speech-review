## Findings (ordered by impact)

1. **The central comparison contradicts the review's own caveat.** The Verdict calls the action "the real contribution" because "90 raw characters per minute ... more than double the 39.2 correct characters per minute". The fourth bullet of "Where the claims outrun the evidence" says raw cpm without editing are not ccpm with editing. The review should either compare like with like, or state the confound right where it uses the number. One option: the paper's free-typing or correct-rate equivalent, about 85 cpm after 5.4% errors (inference). The ratio would survive, but as written the review uses a number it criticizes elsewhere.
2. **The comparison does not separate the action from the decoder, and it misses the authors' own evidence for the action.** The 2017 system [2] used a linear point-and-click decoder; the target uses an RNN. "The action is the real contribution" confounds action with decoder generation. The strongest support for the action's own value is the authors' analysis (Fig. 3) that handwritten letters are more separable than straight-line movements. The review neither cites it nor discusses it.
3. **The M04 usual approach contradicts the Appendix.** The text and table say attempted speech is the most frequent (37 of 123 slot-filled). The Appendix full-text counts on the same 154 studies give imagined or inner speech 68 and attempted speech 56. Either name the measure ("most frequent among slot-filled rows") or report imagined speech as the most frequent named action.
4. **The state of the art for M02 and M12 is out of date, and the sections are inconsistent.** [4] (2023, 23.8% WER) is given as state of the art for M02 and M12, while [7] (2024, 2.5% WER at 125,000 words, also CTC-trained per the evaluator's knowledge) is the state of the art for M06/M07. Use [7] throughout, or explain why [4] is the reference for those slots.
5. **The novelty claim has no named deciding priors.** "None of the 36 studies dated 2020 or earlier names attempted handwriting" relies on a pattern match only. It names no handwriting-decoding prior that was checked. The obvious near-miss is the target's own 2020 bioRxiv preprint titled "imagined handwriting". The pattern should cover "imagined/attempted handwriting, letter writing, character trajectories", and the sentence should say it did. Earlier EEG/ECoG handwriting-trajectory studies may exist (unverified).
6. **The peak is compared with a mean.** "Attempted finger typing at 110 characters per minute [5] ... overtaken it": c29 shows 110 cpm is T18's peak (T17 peaked at 47). The target's 90 cpm is a plateau or mean. Add the condition ("peak, one of two participants"), or use the comparable mean.
7. **"Strongest overreach" is stronger than the evidence.** The Abstract says ">99% accuracy offline with a general-purpose autocorrect". The authors already flag the retrospective LM. The fair criticisms are the unstated LM corpus and the out-of-vocabulary substitution (Extended Data Table 1). Reword it as a framing issue the authors disclose. "Unstated corpus" is itself an "insufficient quote" until the Methods are checked; it is already a question for the authors.
8. **The M07 comparator is outside the slot's inclusion rule.** M07 is defined as a vocabulary of at least 1,000 words, but the concurrent example [8] uses 50 words. Either flag it as a small-vocabulary illustration or replace it.
9. **The M02 "standard" verdict rests on the wrong prior.** The text cites CTC [3] to call an HMM-labeling design standard. Cite the Appendix's own tally (HMM or forced alignment, 9 of 36 studies up to 2020) and name one of those HMM priors.
10. **The M07 usual approach overstates the tally.** A pattern match for "n-gram" (134) cannot tell online from offline language models. Remove "in the decoding loop" or support it with a slot-filled count.
11. **The [6] claim lacks its condition.** The quote "2% character error rate for T18 with a decoder trained ..." is cut off. State whether this was cross-brain transfer, offline or online, and where (section, figure).
12. **Fairness.** The ">2x the previous record" framing in the Verdict and the single-participant limitation (t47) are both stated by the authors. The review should attribute them to the authors, not present them as its own finding.
13. **The Appendix denominators are opaque.** Slot-filled ledgers of 5,595 or 7,345 sit next to "37 of 123" and "61 of 96", and table entries such as "slot-filled 7,329" exceed the 154-study population. One clause per line saying which population each number counts would prevent misreading. (This is not a challenge to the tallies themselves.)
14. **Bibliographic gaps.** Add the volume/pages for [2] (eLife 6:e18554), [3] (J Neural Eng 17(6):066015) and [4] (Nature 620:1031-1036). Verify the DOIs of [5] and [6], which are unverified.
