## Findings (ordered by impact)

**F1. High. Location: M06 and M08 "Usual approach", the slots M06 and M08 n/matched, Verdict "0 of 5 earlier studies", Appendix M06 and M08.**
Evidence: the intracranial production population leaves out the authors' own pilot, Herff et al. 2016 EMBC, pp. 1540-1543, doi:10.1109/EMBC.2016.7591004, PMID 28268620. The PubMed abstract says: "electrocoticography (ECoG) intracranial activity ... can be used to resynthesize speech in real-time. This is accomplished by reconstructing the audio magnitude spectrogram from neural activity and subsequently creating the audio waveform from these reconstructed spectrograms." Angrick 2019 ([3], ref [30]) cites it: "In a pilot study, we showed that it was possible to reconstruct an audio waveform from ECoG signals during speech production". It is in the reference list of a paper the review read in full, yet it is not in the wiki or the audits. The target does not cite it either.
Fix: add it to both prior tallies. M06 becomes regression 6 of 6 (linear or Kalman 4, deep 3) and unit selection 0 of 6. M08 becomes 4 waveform studies: parametric 2, neural vocoder 1, and 1 spectrogram-to-waveform inversion (check the method in the full text before naming it). Rewrite the M08 usual approach as "no common method; 2 of 4 parametric". Its verdict basis then rests on the cross-signal precedent, not on a majority.

**F2. High. Location: M06 paragraph ("40% lower ... 17% lower"); Verdict ("correlates higher"); "Where the claims outrun the evidence" ("is contradicted on the same data").**
Evidence: the two correlations are computed differently on the same recordings. Angrick 2019 Sec. 3 says: "The correlations are computed for each frequency bin individually between the reconstructed spectrogram and its original counterpart". That spectrogram is the DenseNet output, before WaveNet, scored over the whole recording. The target says: "computing correlations between original and generated audio spectrograms ... We calculated the correlations for each word individually", on the resynthesized waveform, with "The set of spoken words in training and test set were always disjoint". The review's "mean 0.41" is cited to quote a6, which is participant 6 ("r 6 = 0.41 ± 0.07"). The true mean, 0.413, matches only by coincidence. Participant numbering across the two papers is not shown to correspond, so "best participant 0.69 against 0.574" may compare different people (still valid as best against best).
Fix: present the result as the authors' own concession plus an unrun comparison, for example: "The authors concede 'slightly lower correlations' than Angrick et al. on the same recordings (Discussion). The reported values (DenseNet log-mel, per bin: mean of six 0.41, best 0.69; target resynthesized audio, per word, word-disjoint folds: mean 0.246, best 0.574) are not computed the same way, so no same-protocol comparison exists." Either drop the 40% and 17% figures or keep them only with "(inference; different correlation definitions)". In "Where the claims outrun", change "is contradicted on the same data" to "is not supported: the only same-data decoder reports higher, differently computed correlations, and the authors concede 'slightly lower'". Cite the mean as computed from a1-a6 (inference), not from a6.

**F3. High. Location: reference [4] and every [4] citation (Verdict, M06, M08, "Where the claims outrun").**
Evidence: the wiki folder doi-10.1109-taslp-2017-2738568 holds Matthias Janke's KIT dissertation, "EMG-to-Speech: Direct Generation of Speech from Facial Electromyographic Signals" (oral exam 22.04.2016; reviewers Schultz and Black). It is not the TASLP 2017 article by Janke & Diener, 25(12):2375-2385. The quoted passages come from dissertation chapters: Ch. 7.1 cosine target cost; Ch. 9 "Comparative Evaluation"; Fig. 9.4 preference test; Table 9.1 real-time factor. The claim-table locations "Sec. 9 (listening test)" and "Sec. 7.1" are dissertation chapter numbers; the TASLP article is too short to have a Sec. 9. No one has checked whether the TASLP article contains the four-way comparison, the "never preferred" preference result or the real-time factor table.
Fix: cite the dissertation as [4] (Janke, M., KIT, 2016; take the identifier from the PDF or KITopen). Alternatively, obtain the TASLP text and re-quote from it. Do not keep the TASLP bibliographic entry next to quotes taken from the dissertation.

**F4. Medium. Location: Appendix M06 ("one 2019 workshop report mentions the authors' talk"), Verdict, dating.**
Evidence: pmc-7539697 shows that Herff presented unit selection from ECoG in "inferior frontal, motor and pre-motor cortices", together with "specifically tailored deep neural networks" (the Angrick DenseNet), at the 7th BCI Meeting on 21-25 May 2018. That is before both 2018-11 bioRxiv preprints. The target was received on 3 May 2019. I found no bioRxiv or abstract-book version of the target (unverified either way). Under a strict reading, the target's idea was first public in 2018-05, and the DenseNet decoder was a sibling method shown in the same talk rather than independent prior work. This does not invalidate the M06 and M08 priors, which are all earlier than 2018-05 except Angrick and Anumanchipalli. It does change how the DenseNet gap should be framed.
Fix: add one sentence noting that the method and the DenseNet were presented together in May 2018 (pmc-7539697) and that the DenseNet paper uses the same data from the same group. The same-data gap is then the authors' own unrun comparison of their two methods (this supports the F2 fix). Keep the 2019-11 date as the first written version, and state that no earlier written version was found.

**F5. Medium. Location: Verdict ("against the paper's claims of superiority and speed"); M08 paragraph.**
Evidence: the target's speed claim is a measurement on its own inventory: "less than 1ms for each new window every 10 ms ... for our limited dataset size". Its comparison with DNNs concerns training cost: "does not require the huge computational costs of deep neural networks". The EMG run-time result (dissertation Table 9.1) is for EMG codebooks of different sizes, and the dissertation itself says "times are heavily related to the amount of training/codebook data". It does not contradict a speed that was measured on another inventory. The same applies to listener preference: EMG unit selection ranking last is cross-signal evidence about naturalness, not a measurement of this system.
Fix: in the Verdict, say that EMG evidence "suggests unit-selection cost grows with the inventory and that listeners preferred DNN output in EMG [4]", and keep "against the paper's claims" only for "superior results". In "Where the claims outrun", drop "and listeners rated EMG unit selection lowest [4]" or mark it as cross-signal context.

**F6. Medium. Location: M08 and M12 "State of the art", summary table rows M08 and M12.**
Evidence: (a) The M08 slot is how features become a waveform. Brain2voice 2.0's 5.24% WER is a full-pipeline intelligibility score on an intracortical benchmark and comes from a bioRxiv preprint. It does not isolate the waveform step, and the preprint status is not stated. (b) The M12 state of the art gives Wairagkar 2025's median 43.75% WER as the best transcription result, yet the review's own M08 line reports a later 5.24% WER on the same benchmark. The two state-of-the-art lines contradict each other.
Fix: (a) In M08, cite a state-of-the-art waveform method, for example the best own-voice or neural-vocoder result whose paper attributes it to the vocoder, or keep Brain2voice 2.0 with "(full system, preprint)". (b) In M12, either cite Brain2voice 2.0 (5.24% listener transcription WER, 2026, preprint, same benchmark) as the transcription state of the art, or state that the slot measures test design (open transcription of the full test set) and use Wairagkar 2025's 956-sentence open transcription as the design exemplar.

**F7. Medium. Location: M12 "Usual approach"; slots M12 "smallest choice set among neural precedents"; Appendix M12.**
Evidence: M12 counts 13 studies across all non-acoustic signals, 11 of them non-neural, plus Akbari 2019, which is a perception study (excluded from M06 as "perception"). The comparison of choice-set sizes uses one production precedent (Anumanchipalli). The setting therefore differs from M06 and M08, and "four options is fewer than any choice size in the precedent" rests on a single study. The audit labels the 13 "confirmed" in `prior_to_target` and "pattern match" in `usual_approach_at_target_time`, but the review prints "confirmed 13".
Fix: say "13 studies across EMG, ultrasound, EMA, video and neural signals (2 neural, 1 of them perception)". Phrase the choice-set point as "fewer than the 10, 25 and 50 choices of the only earlier neural production test [6] and the 10-digit test of the neural perception study (inference: digits 0-9)". Verify the last point or drop it. Make the tier label consistent.

**F8. Low. Location: M12 paragraph, first sentence ("The paper states the design plainly: 55 listeners, four-word forced choice ...").**
Evidence: this is a restatement of the method, and "states ... plainly" is a remark about the paper, not a finding. M06 ("The target instead picks the nearest of over 50,000 stored frames") and M08 ("The target overlap-adds 150 ms windows") also restate the method, though as contrast lines they are borderline.
Fix: open M12 with the finding: "Chance is 25% and the 30 stimuli are the best-correlated words; mean 66.1% (Fig. 5B) is 55% of the way from chance to ceiling (inference) and holds only for those words." Fold the listener count into that sentence.

**F9. Low. Location: M06 paragraph ("Word-disjoint folds and waveform-level scoring may explain part of it (inference)").**
Evidence: this hedge is the decisive confound for F2, not a side remark, and the texts allow a plain statement (see F2).
Fix: replace it with a plain statement of the metric difference, with both locations (Angrick Sec. 3; target Correlation Analysis).

**F10. Low. Location: Verdict; slots M06 verdict "standard".**
Evidence: the verdict calls the design "new to intracranial synthesis (0 of 5)" and still rates it "standard" because of cross-signal precedent. That is defensible, but the deciding priors for the cross-signal case (Hueber 2007/2008/2010; Zahner 2014, Diener 2015, Janke) appear only in the audit, not in the Appendix text.
Fix: name the 6 biosignal unit-selection priors in the Appendix by short citation and year, so readers can check the "standard" rating.

**F11. Low. Location: claims.md row 10; Questions for the authors.**

Fix: add "(apart from a qualitative comparison with Martin et al., Discussion)" to the M06 sentence about the in-paper baseline.
