# ASR is all you need: cross-modal distillation for lip reading

Afouras, T., Chung, J. S., Zisserman, A. · ICASSP 2020 · 2020 · [arXiv:1911.12747](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12747)

## Verdict

The paper [[1]](#ref-1) changes the training labels (M02): an audio-only ASR teacher supplies transcripts and frame posteriors for untranscribed video. Earlier lip readers took audio-derived frame targets only from transcribed data [[2]](#ref-2); none of 77 matches ASR labels or distillation (pattern match, Appendix). On LRS2 the no-label arm matches ground truth (58.2% against 58.5%, Table 3), which supports the authors' parity claim. It stays standard, not ahead: the label effect differs across datasets (0.3 points on LRS2, 3.2 on LRS3), each one WER with no run count, and no row separates distillation from CTC on ASR labels. The public-data state-of-the-art claim (M12) rests on 0.3- and 0.4-point margins over the [concurrent] Conv-seq2seq [[3]](#ref-3) (inference).

## Against the field, component by component

### M02 Training-only supervision: ASR-teacher labels on untranscribed video

**Usual approach:** Before 2019-05, none of 77 sentence-level studies matches ASR-generated labels or teacher distillation (pattern match, absence count, Appendix). The closest designs take frame targets from an audio ASR's forced alignment on transcribed data: Sari et al. for a visual CNN [[2]](#ref-2), Tatulli and Hueber for video and ultrasound [[4]](#ref-4). The paper drops the transcript and uses soft posteriors.

**State of the art:** USR 2.0 (2026) [[5]](#ref-5) [later] reaches 17.6% LRS3 visual-only WER with 656 h human-labelled and 2,649 h self-labelled video, the best without an external LM or synthetic video; Auto-AVSR (2023) [[6]](#ref-6) [later] reaches 19.1% with external-ASR labels. The paper's 61.7% without human labels (LRS3, Table 3) is 44.1 points higher, a gap that mixes label regime, model and data scale (inference).

No wiki study before 2019-05 (2,648 with full text) distils an audio or ASR teacher into a decoder for another signal (Appendix). LIBS [[7]](#ref-7) [concurrent] distils a recognizer into a lip reader but aligns it with "the ground truth sentence", so it needs transcripts (65.29% LRS2 WER); a throat-microphone student of a close-talk teacher [[8]](#ref-8) [concurrent] is adjacent, as its student is acoustic. The authors call Wei et al.'s AVSR distillation "the closest work to ours"; its teacher is trained on the student's labelled data, so it "does not apply naturally to unlabelled audio-visual data" (§1.1).

The authors report "similar performance on LRS2 (58.2%) and even better on LRS3 (65.6%)" against 58.5% and 68.8% for CTC on ground truth (§5, Table 3): 0.5% and 4.7% relative (inference). Both arms change labels and loss; CTC on ASR transcripts (§1) has no WER. Clean VoxCeleb2 lowers LRS3 WER from 65.6% to 61.7% (5.9% relative) (inference); the LRS2 drop to 54.2% also adds ASR-labelled LRS3 (Table 3). Verdict: standard, as inconsistent single-WER effects do not reach ahead.

### M12 Evaluation: public-data claim without run counts

**Usual approach:** Neither of 2 earlier LRS3 visual-only studies states seeds or repeated runs (pattern match, Appendix); V2P adds bootstrap test errors [[9]](#ref-9). The paper gives one WER per row, no run count, and no comparator decoders or language models (Table 3).

**State of the art:** USR 2.0 (2026) [[5]](#ref-5) [later] 17.6% without external LM or synthetic video; SynthVSR 16.9% with both (its Table 7); LP Conformer (arXiv 2023; ICASSP 2024) [[10]](#ref-10) [later] 12.8% with 100k h of non-public video.

The authors claim: "we obtain state-of-the-art results on the challenging LRS2 and LRS3 datasets for training only on publicly available data" (Abstract), and concede that proprietary-data systems remain better and that Conv-seq2seq is "very close to ours" (§6). The margins, 0.4 points on LRS2 (51.3% against 51.7%) and 0.3 on LRS3 (59.8% against 60.1%), are smaller than the bootstrap standard error of one system on the full LRS3 test set reported by V2P, ±0.9 [[9]](#ref-9) (inference). TM-seq2seq, trained with non-public MV-LRS, gives 59.9% without and 58.9% with its external LM [[11]](#ref-11); the paper's 59.8% uses a 6-gram LM. Verdict: standard.

## Where the claims outrun the evidence

- The abstract says the teacher was trained "on a large-scale audio-only corpus" (Librispeech, §4.1); "without requiring human annotated ground truth data" holds for video only, as transcripts move to the teacher's audio and the LM text.
- "We show how arbitrary amounts of unlabelled video data can be leveraged to improve performance" (Abstract): §6 concedes "Given resource constraints we only" used VoxCeleb2. The test is one 334 h subset kept by two ASR-based filters (90% or more valid English among teacher-output words of 4+ characters; under 28% WER between teacher and a second recognizer; §2), with no unfiltered control and no teacher WER.
- Faster training than CTC on ASR labels (§1) is shown only against CTC on ground truth (Fig. 2).

## What the field needs next

- M02: ground-truth against ASR labels crossed with CTC against CTC plus distillation, fixed data and model, with teacher WER.
- M02: unfiltered against filtered VoxCeleb2 at equal hours.
- M12: repeated runs or bootstrap errors [[9]](#ref-9), comparators decoded with one LM.
- M11: a speaker overlap audit between VoxCeleb2 and the LRS2/LRS3 test sets.

## Questions for the authors

- What WER does CTC on ASR transcripts alone reach on LRS2 and LRS3?
- What is the teacher's WER on LRS2, LRS3 and the clean VoxCeleb2 subset?
- How many runs and seeds underlie each Table 3 row?
