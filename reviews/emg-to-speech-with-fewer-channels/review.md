# EMG-to-Speech with Fewer Channels

Hwang, Lee, Lee. arXiv preprint, 2026. [arXiv:2602.06460v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06460v1)

## Verdict

The paper [[1]](#ref-1) changes sensing (M05, exhaustive 4-of-8 search) and training (M03, full-model initialization plus channel dropout); both are standard, and evaluation (M12) is behind. Its 8 → 4 cost, +35% relative, repeats EMG recognition (+32% [[2]](#ref-2), +36% [[3]](#ref-3)), and exhaustive EMG subset search exists [[4]](#ref-4): synthesis confirms a known cost. Initialization helps at 4–6 electrodes; channel dropout does not (p=0 is best at 6 and 4), yet the introduction and conclusion credit it. The main confound is an unrepeated ~35% baseline, 8–10 points worse than the same lab's later reports [[5]](#ref-5), [[6]](#ref-6): it inflates the absolute cost and leaves the 2.4-point top-10 spread inside seed noise. Virtual deletion cannot support device guidance.

## Against the field, component by component

### M05 Sensing: electrode count and subset selection

**Usual approach:** rank sensors individually or greedily and report the cost (53 confirmed works); the paper adds a retrained exhaustive 4-of-8 search.

**State of the art:** all 2,047 subsets of 11 EMG sensors scored on WER (Meltzner 2018 [[4]](#ref-4)); held-out selection [[7]](#ref-7) [later] and seeds [[8]](#ref-8) [later]. The paper matches the search, lacks both, and cites none of [[2]](#ref-2), [[3]](#ref-3), [[4]](#ref-4).

Few-sensor parity in classification ([[9]](#ref-9), [[10]](#ref-10), [[11]](#ref-11), [[12]](#ref-12), [[13]](#ref-13), [[14]](#ref-14) [later]; not [[15]](#ref-15)) comes from redundant 64–203-channel arrays. Continuous decoding pays steadily: WER ×0.57 per doubling of intracortical electrodes [[16]](#ref-16); about 16% for Meltzner 2018's best four against 10.4% for the best eight. Ranking-versus-combination divergence ([[17]](#ref-17), [[18]](#ref-18); not [[14]](#ref-14)) is layout-specific. Standard.

### M03 Training: channel masking and transfer across layouts

**Usual approach:** random channel masking (9 confirmed works) and per-layout input layers or position embeddings (14); the paper's resized first convolution is the minimal day-specific input layer of [[16]](#ref-16).

**State of the art:** removing locked channel dropout raises WER 21.6% → 26.4% (MoDAl [[19]](#ref-19) [later]); [[20]](#ref-20) [concurrent] tests 162 deletions without retraining, where channel-drop augmentation slightly hurt. The paper never tests deletion without retraining, as [[20]](#ref-20) and [[21]](#ref-21) [later] do.

At p=0 initialization gains about 3.7, 3.9 and 2.6 points at 6, 5 and 4 electrodes, about four times SVRA's seed SD [[6]](#ref-6); with no variance and a weak baseline, extra training of an under-converged model explains it equally well. Dropout ("widely adopted", §3.2) wins only at 5. Standard.

### M12 Evaluation: repeats, selection, baseline

**Usual approach:** synthesis papers rarely state repeats (1 of 425); the paper ranks single numbers.

**State of the art:** repeats ([[16]](#ref-16), [[8]](#ref-8) [later], [[21]](#ref-21) [later]), held-out selection ([[22]](#ref-22), [[7]](#ref-7)), 10 seeds on this corpus ([[6]](#ref-6) [later]), listeners [[23]](#ref-23).

Same-pipeline baselines: 25.12% [[5]](#ref-5), 26.75 ± 0.99% [[6]](#ref-6), 25.74% [[24]](#ref-24); ~35% is 8.3 SVRA SDs above. Behind.

**Incidental.** M04: others report silent and voiced apart ([[16]](#ref-16), [[18]](#ref-18), [[20]](#ref-20), [[25]](#ref-25)). M07: Whisper [[26]](#ref-26) adds its own language model to scoring. M13: others recorded a reduced layout [[2]](#ref-2).

## Where the claims outrun the evidence

- **Dropout.** §1 ("yields additional performance gains") and §6 ("consistently improved") versus Fig. 3: p>0 beats p=0 only at 5 electrodes (38.1% vs 39.5%); at 4, p=0.25 is ~1.9 points worse. The abstract and §5.4 are accurate; the gains are initialization's.
- **Ranking and the 5-vs-6 reversal (§5.4).** The top 10 span 2.4 points (one point ≈ 16 words on ~100 utterances, [[18]](#ref-18) Table 2); 43.4% vs 42.9% from scratch and 38.1% vs 39.2% fine-tuned sit inside SVRA's ~1-point seed SD [[6]](#ref-6), so the "interaction" claim rests on single runs.
- **Inconsistencies.** §5.2 ranks "3, 2, 5, 1, …" but Table 2 (ch1 52.6%, ch5 52.8%) gives 3, 2, 1, 5; submental ch3 is put "near the sternohyoid", an infrahyoid muscle (genioglossus in [[25]](#ref-25) §4.2).
- **Table 3 "PER".** 16.0% overall, below vowels (22.6%) and consonants (20.9%), is a frame average with silence, which alone moves frame accuracy 84.68% → 52.74% on this corpus [[30]](#ref-30) [later]. A 16.0% → 17.1% rise (ch8), unrepeated, does not make channels 7 and 8 "key".
- **Silent vs voiced.** The throat electrode leads voiced pitch and loudness [[25]](#ref-25), dropping it improved voiced-to-silent transfer (human WER 88.8% → 64.6% [[18]](#ref-18)), and silent speech loses less to missing channels [[20]](#ref-20): ch4's rank depends on the unstated test mode.
- **Prior subsets.** Lee et al.'s subset is given as (1,2,3,5), 5th at 48.4%; it is 2,3,4,6 ([[25]](#ref-25) §4.4), outside the top 10.

## What the field needs next

1. M12: retrain the 70 subsets with 5–10 seeds, split variance as in [[8]](#ref-8), select on validation, test once, from a 25–27% baseline.
2. M03: test the dropout-pretrained model on reduced inputs without retraining, ablated as in [[19]](#ref-19); fit an additive per-channel model (R² near Scheck's ≈0.87 [[20]](#ref-20) undercuts complementarity).
3. M04/M11: score subsets on silent and voiced tests, and with listeners [[23]](#ref-23) instead of Whisper.
4. M13: record with four electrodes only, across re-donning ([[18]](#ref-18) §2.2).

## Questions for the authors

1. Is the test EMG silent or voiced, and how is Whisper output normalized?
2. How many seeds per subset, and which data chose (1,3,5,6)?
3. Why ~35% at 8 electrodes against 25–27% in your later papers?
4. How are weights moved to the resized first convolution? Is skipping 1/(1−p) rescaling (§3.2) intended?
5. Why 16 hours, when [[18]](#ref-18) Table 2 gives 18.6? Why are [[2]](#ref-2), [[3]](#ref-3), [[4]](#ref-4) uncited?
