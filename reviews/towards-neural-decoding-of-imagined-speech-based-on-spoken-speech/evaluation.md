## Numbered findings (ordered by impact)

1. **Contradiction on the statistics (C6).** The review says no pairwise test exists, but the review's own claim table quotes the paper stating a post-hoc non-parametric bootstrap analysis. Rewrite as: a post-hoc bootstrap test is stated (§II.D), but its pairwise results for transfer against baseline are not reported. This matters because the M12 critique and the "Comparable performance" critique both rest on it.
2. **Per-subject misreading (C3).** The two lowest baselines are S5 (23.7) and S6 (27.3); S4 (28.7) is third. The full-trial transfer beats the baseline in S4 and S5. The few-trial transfer beats it in S5 and S6, with S6 gaining +7.0 points. Name the model and fix the ranking, or drop the sentence.
3. **Visual-imagery argument incomplete (C7).** "Only the baselines differ" (Verdict) and "comes from its higher baseline" (outrun section) leave out that few-trial transfer is 2.4 points lower for visual imagery (25.6 vs 28.0). That gap is larger than the 1.3-point baseline gap. The conclusion that the authors' comparison does not support their claim still holds, because the full-trial transfer is nearly identical (26.8 vs 26.3). But the explanation must name both differences.
4. **Appendix vs Verdict chronology contradiction (C14).** Rekrut et al. (SMC 2022, scalp EEG) is cited as [concurrent], but the Appendix lists the single concurrent study as sEEG and says all scalp-EEG studies are later. Reconcile the two. If the target and Rekrut really are the first scalp-EEG cases in the ledger, the review should say so plainly. At present it credits the target with nothing in M04 while its own ledger implies the signal departure is early.
5. **M06 state of the art lacks evidence and is questionable (C13).** The 96.4% four-class covert-EEG figure for Jahangiri & Sepulveda (2019) has no claim-table row. A 2019 number is also not "today". Very high covert-EEG accuracies from that line of work are widely questioned for block or temporal leakage under within-session CV (unverified for this paper). Either quote it with its CV condition and a caveat, or remove it.
6. **M04 "state of the art today" is a non-peer-reviewed negative result (C11, C12).** Luporini (2026, Zenodo technical report) is a leakage-control study with near-chance transfer in two subjects. It is a useful methodological comparator, but it is not evidence of the best current performance. Label it as the most rigorous control rather than as the state of the art, or name better peer-reviewed later work. The 6.7% → 9.0% figures need metric, task and modality, or should be cut.
7. **Untested direction stated as fact (C4).** "10 spoken trials per class do better" should be stated as an untested 1.2-point mean difference.
8. **M12 tally used without its recall caveat (C15).** The Appendix reports known-item recall 0.0 for the M12 topic. The component text calls the paper's practice "the majority" with no caveat. Add one clause noting that the tally is a pattern match with zero known-item recall.
9. **"No chance comparison" wording (C16).** The review itself compares 26.8% with 20% chance. Rephrase: without the test-trial count, the significance of 26.8% against 20% chance cannot be assessed.
10. **Missing or thin quotes (C9, C10, C17).** These items need claim-table quotes: Rekrut's 15 participants and 5 commands, "resting-state" for Martin's control, and the "actual vocalizations" phrase from §IV.
11. **Possible earlier scalp-EEG overt-to-imagined work (novelty check, unverified).** Cooney, Folli and Coyle, "Optimizing Layers Improves CNN Generalization and Transfer Learning for Imagined Speech Decoding from EEG" (IEEE SMC 2019), used overt-speech EEG for transfer to imagined speech. I believe that design is pre-training plus fine-tuning, which the inclusion rule excludes unless an overt-only condition is reported. Check whether it reports one before keeping "none uses scalp EEG" among prior works.
12. **Minor bookkeeping.** Claim row 17 (the M12 verdict) cites the M04 audit as its location. Table I subject 7's transfer values (26.8, 28.0) equal the column means exactly. This is consistent with the means I recomputed, but worth a second look at the extraction.

Position check: the central position is that transfer is below the within-imagined baseline, that "comparable" is not shown, and that the visual-imagery claim is unsupported. Table I and the test statistics support it. The strongest confound, which the review handles, is the different evaluation splits: a random 90/10 split within imagined data against cross-paradigm transfer, with sessions not stated. Findings 1 and 3 should be fixed before the position is stated as firmly as "fails".
