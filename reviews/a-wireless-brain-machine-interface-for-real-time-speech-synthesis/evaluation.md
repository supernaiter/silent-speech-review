## Required fixes, ordered by impact

1. **High: unsupported absence and novelty claims (deciding priors neither named nor read).** The Verdict says "the ledger holds no earlier closed loop from a non-acoustic signal (Appendix)". M06 says "this paper sets the design". M08 says "the only ones driven by brain signals are this paper and work by the same group". SKILL.md (lines 111, 150, 163) requires the deciding priors to be named and read. Here the 5 pre-2009 hits were excluded on titles only (M06 audit note), 17 of 87 have unresolved dates, `read_prior` holds only [4] (a [later] work) and a PMC version of the target itself, and no pre-2009 work was read. Possible earlier designs from outside the ledger (unverified, borderline for the inclusion rule): Fels and Hinton, Glove-Talk (IEEE Trans. Neural Networks 4(1), 1993) and Glove-TalkII (1998). Both map a non-acoustic signal (hand gestures) to a formant synthesizer in real time with closed-loop audio. Fix: either read and name the deciding priors (the 5 pre-2009 studies, resolve the 17 undated, and decide on Glove-Talk under the rule), or remove "sets the design" and "no earlier closed loop", and name the same-group works in the M08 "only" sentence. The verdicts stay `standard` either way, so this is a wording and evidence fix, not a verdict change.
2. **High: the M13 state of the art does not match the design dimension, and the "2 against 192" comparison has different starting points.** The target's M13 property is a closed skin: no percutaneous hardware. [5] keeps the percutaneous pedestal and puts a transmitter on it. "Kept the skin closed at a cost of 2 channels against 192 [5]" therefore compares a skin-closed system with one that is not. The ledger tally itself counts 51 "fully implanted" studies. The state of the art should be the best skin-closed communication BCI, for example (all [later]; values unverified here): Vansteensel et al. 2016 NEJM, a fully implanted ECoG communication BCI used at home; Oxley et al. 2021 J NeuroIntervent Surg, Stentrode, fully implanted; Benabid et al. 2019 Lancet Neurol, WIMAGINE, 64-channel fully implanted wireless; Neuralink N1 (1,024 channels; check for a peer-reviewed report). Keep [5] only as the best wireless-on-pedestal system, and recompute the channel comparison against a skin-closed system.
3. **Medium: crediting the paper with "changing the link into transcutaneous telemetry".** The review itself asks how the "first telemetric implant" claim relates to refs 32 and 33 (Kennedy et al. 2000, 2004: computer control with the same Neurotrophic Electrode). If those implants already sent signals by FM telemetry through intact skin, which is my recollection but unverified, then the M13 change is the group's earlier design, not this paper's. The Verdict sentence then overstates. Fix: check refs 32/33 (or Kennedy & Bakay 1998, NeuroReport) and either limit the M13 change to "first use of the telemetric implant for speech or audio output" or keep the claim with a located quote.
4. **Medium: the 13-components table shows slot-filled counts, not design counts.** Rows M01–M05, M07 and M09–M12 give "X noted / recorded (slot-filled N)", for example M05 "Sensors recorded (slot-filled 7,329)" and M04 "Use-time action noted (slot-filled 5,595)". These count how often the slot is filled, not how often a design occurs, so the "Usual approach (count)" column holds no usual approach for 10 of 13 rows. SKILL.md line 112 also asks that sparse slots M02 and M07 use pattern-match counts. Replace each with the most frequent design value and its count and tier, or with "–" plus a pointer to `slot-notes.md`.
5. **Medium: counting bases are inconsistent between the text, the table and the Appendix.**
   - (a) M13 text: "35 percutaneous, 52 wireless, 51 fully implanted, *the last two* upper bounds". The Appendix marks all three as "(pattern match, upper bounds)". The table then names the smallest of the three (35) as the "usual approach" without justification.
   - (b) M13 Appendix: "216 of 296 (slot-filled)" has a denominator (296) that matches neither 382 nor 6,075 and is not explained.
   - (c) M06: the population of 87 is described as studies that meet the inclusion rule, yet 24 are counted as "offline whole-utterance decoding", a design the rule excludes. The 87 is therefore a pattern population, not a rule-filtered one, and the text should say so.
   - (d) M08: the Appendix mixes the published neural vocoder 51 with a formant count of 9 from a rerun that gives neural vocoder 54. Use one run.
   - (e) M06 Appendix: the slot-filled "generate 24" and "linear model 12" are shown without their denominator (`population_unique` 84).
6. **Medium: M09 verdict contradicts its own cell.** M09 is "not applicable" with the cell "Audio; cursor late". SKILL.md defines not_applicable as outside the study type, for example confirmation in an offline study. This study is online, with auditory and (in later sessions) visual feedback. Use `standard` or `not_reported`, and check M10 the same way.
7. **Low to medium: misattributions to the Discussion.** See claims 12 and 13. "Two dimensions made the task learnable" is the reviewer's inference, so mark it (inference). The authors concede the vowel limitation, not "voicing or pitch". Restate the latter as following from Methods (all parameters except F1/F2 fixed, text.txt 200–204).
8. **Low: confound handling for the central learning claim is partial.** Sessions had 1–4 blocks (text.txt 303). A 1-block session contributes the same block as both "first" and "last", and later block positions average fewer, possibly better, sessions. The review asks about block counts but does not name this selection effect as the confound on "45% → 70%". One clause in "Where the claims outrun the evidence" would cover it.
9. **Low: Fig. 6 versus cross-validated r.** These are different conditions (one recording, ridge, whole data, against eight sessions, two-fold CV, averaged; text.txt 228–234, 276–280). Say so, or drop the implied overfitting reading.
10. **Low: [2] metric wording and source.** Write "median 43.75% WER (open transcription)". Take the closed-loop latency from [2] itself (synthesis within 10 ms; end-to-end in Extended Data Fig. 1c) rather than from [3]'s 25 ms summary, or keep [3] and note that the two delays are measured differently.
11. **Low: M06 state of the art, possible omission.** Name or rule out Littlejohn et al. 2025, Nature Neuroscience ("A streaming brain-to-voice neuroprosthesis", ECoG, closed-loop streaming synthesis; [later]; metric unverified here) and Angrick et al. 2024, Sci. Rep. (online ALS word synthesis; in the ledger, 75–84% listener recognition). Neither beats 5.24%, but both are closed-loop comparators beside [2].
12. **Low: "The field moved to vocoder features instead of a larger formant space (51 of 180)"** asserts a trend with an undated count. Either give a dated split or say "the field uses".
13. **Low: [later] tag missing** on [2] in the M06 state-of-the-art line.
14. **Low, audit hygiene, not in the review text:** the M06 audit `read_fulltext` contains a stray entry for another review's target (`arxiv-2609.04455`, role "target") and lists a PMC version of this target (`pmc-2784218-wirelesssynth`) as a "prior". `reader2/pmc-13375540-ltbci.txt` is empty. The notes entry c5 condition says "956 evaluation sentences" for the open-transcription WER (it should be 90 trials).

Verdict rules: no slot is `ahead` or `behind`, so the repetition and ≥3-prior requirements are not triggered. Calling M06/M08/M13 `standard` for lack of repetition (one participant, one electrode) follows the rule and judges the protocol, not the results.
