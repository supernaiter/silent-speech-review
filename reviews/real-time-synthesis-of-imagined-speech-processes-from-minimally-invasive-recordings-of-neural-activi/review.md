# Real-time synthesis of imagined speech processes from minimally invasive recordings of neural activity

Angrick M, Ottenhoff MC, Diener L, Ivucic D, Ivucic G, Goulis S, Saal J, Colon AJ, Wagner L, Krusienski DJ, Kubben PL, Schultz T, Herff C. Communications Biology 4, 1055 (2021). [doi:10.1038/s42003-021-02578-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-021-02578-0)

## Verdict

The paper [[1]](#ref-1) changes two components, closed-loop spectral word synthesis from sEEG (M06) and online use of an audible-trained decoder for whispered and imagined words (M04); both are standard, with 5 prior closed loops and 8 prior overt-to-silent transfer studies, Martin et al. among them [[2]](#ref-2) (Appendix). The combination rests on one participant and three short runs, with no repetition across participants or sessions. The evaluation is behind the group's own earlier four-option listener test [[3]](#ref-3): imagined output is scored only by DTW correlation against a null from a separate non-speech session (M12). The main claim, "models trained on audible speech can be used to synthesize imagined speech processes in real-time" (Results), outruns this design, because no control separates imagery from prompt reading and no test (Appendix, M12) shows that the output carries the imagined word.

## Against the field, component by component

### M06 Decoding: closed-loop synthesis

**Usual approach:** At the target's time, 6 of 44 prior pattern-match studies synthesized audio online or under simulated causal inference, 5 as closed loops with playback (Appendix): formant synthesis from intracortical units [[4]](#ref-4) or EEG motor imagery [[5]](#ref-5), and articulatory synthesis from articulography during silent articulation [[6]](#ref-6). The paper departs by outputting a 40-bin word spectrogram, not two formants.

**State of the art:** Intracortical voice synthesis decoded causally within 10 ms reached a median 43.75% open-transcription WER in one participant with ALS [[7]](#ref-7) (2025) [later]; a chronic ECoG closed loop reached 80% listener word recognition [[8]](#ref-8) (2024) [later]; both use attempted speech.

The authors say of Guenther 2009 that "this study did not attempt to synthesize speech beyond basic vowel sounds" (Introduction), which is accurate, and 7.331 ms of processing per 10 ms frame (Table 1) supports the real-time claim. The claim that "two major issues remain unaddressed in the current literature" (Introduction) omits the uncited closed loops [[6]](#ref-6), [[5]](#ref-5) (References). Verdict: standard; whispered and imagined runs of one person are conditions, not repetition.

### M04 Use-time action: imagined words with an audible-trained decoder

**Usual approach:** Applying an overt-trained decoder without retraining to silent, mouthed or covert speech is the confirmed usual design, 8 of 21 prior studies (Appendix): EMG [[9]](#ref-9), ultrasound and video, and ECoG for mimed [[10]](#ref-10) and covert speech [[2]](#ref-2). The paper runs it online.

**State of the art:** A Transformer trained only on overt ECoG reached 46.3% token error on covert sentences across 16 patients [[11]](#ref-11) (2024) [later], offline.

Transfer is small: imagined trials reached a DTW correlation of 0.32 ± 0.26 against a 0.17 ± 0.22 null, below whispered trials at 0.64 ± 0.15 (Results), 0.15 above the null on 75 trials of one person (inference). Verdict: standard; the method is Martin et al.'s [[2]](#ref-2) run in closed loop.

### M12 Evaluation: identification of the synthesized word

**Usual approach:** Of 7 prior reconstructions of produced, mimed or imagined speech, 4 report correlation only and 3 add identification or a listener test (Appendix): pairwise identification of covert reconstructions [[2]](#ref-2), a four-option listener test at 66.1% against 25% chance [[3]](#ref-3), and listener transcription at a median 31% WER with a 25-word pool [[10]](#ref-10).

**State of the art:** 80% listener recognition of online-synthesized words [[8]](#ref-8) (2024) [later]; 92-100% word identification from reconstructed spectrograms [[12]](#ref-12) (2023) [later].

The authors drop STOI because trials may lack 400 ms of consecutive speech and state that the audio is not intelligible (Results); that explains the missing listener test, not the missing identification test, which the 250-word list and matched overt references make direct. Verdict: behind, against the best comparable work, Anumanchipalli et al. [[10]](#ref-10), read in full.

## Where the claims outrun the evidence

- The closed-loop null is 1,000 2-s segments from a separate grasping and eye-fixation session (Fig. 4): it separates speech-task activity from another task, not one imagined word from others.
- The decoder relies most on high-gamma 150-200 ms before the current frame over inferior and middle frontal cortex (Fig. 5). A visual prompt precedes every trial, so prompt reading can drive output without imagery (inference); more detected speech in imagined than whispered intertrial intervals (Fig. 4) agrees.
- The causal design excludes her own voice (Fig. 4, Results), but she heard the synthesized output; without a feedback-off imagined run, responses to it inside the 200 ms context are not separated from imagery (inference).
- The whispered reference is her audible articulation of the word, her whisper being below microphone sensitivity (Fig. 4, Results).
- The imagined 0.32 ± 0.26 is called a mean, but the Fig. 4 script computes medians; the Fig. 3 test states 100 chance values where the script pools 1,000; the VAD settings (threshold 4, scaling 0.5) differ from config/evaluation.ini.

## What the field needs next

- M12: closed-set identification of imagined output over the 250-word list, against a mismatched-word null.
- M04: compare audible-trained and imagined-adapted decoders across participants and sessions.
- M06: alternate feedback-on and feedback-off imagined blocks.

## Questions for the authors

- How much time separated the training run from the closed-loop runs?
- Is the imagined 0.32 ± 0.26 a mean or a median, and which n2 underlies the open-loop test?
- Was a reading-only or prompt-without-imagery condition recorded?
- What is the latency from neural sample to loudspeaker?

## Appendix: literature search

- M06, causal-closed-loop-speech-synthesis. Inclusion rule: Studies that synthesize speech acoustics from a non-acoustic signal (neural, EMG, articulography, ultrasound, video) online, in real time, with the audio played back to the user while or right after they speak. Boundary: include formant or vocoder synthesis of vowels, words or sentences, and offline studies only when they simulate causal frame-level inference; exclude offline acausal reconstruction, text decoding read out by TTS, and conversion from acoustic input; count versions once. Ledger counts: slot-filled 7,345 unique studies; pattern match 87 unique studies (all years). At the target's time (first public before 2021-03): 44 prior pattern-match unique studies (target excluded, versions merged); 6 meet the rule, 5 of them closed loops with playback (intracortical 1, articulography 3, scalp EEG 1); [concurrent] 2021-03 to 2022-03: 0 meeting the rule. Read in full: [[4]](#ref-4), [[6]](#ref-6), [[5]](#ref-5), [[7]](#ref-7). Known-item recall 1.0 (4 seeds).
- M04, overt-to-silent-transfer. Inclusion rule: Include studies that apply a decoder trained only on overt (or attempted overt) speech to silent speech (mouthed, silently articulated, inner or imagined speech) without retraining and report the results. Any signal qualifies (neural, EMG, ultrasound, video, etc.). Boundary: those that also train on silent data (such as methods that transfer audible-side targets) are included if they separately report an audible-training-only baseline condition. Exclude those that use an audible model only for time alignment or initialization of silent data. Exclude transfer between whispered and normal voice in acoustic speech. Exclude transfer from listening (perception) to inner speech. Exclude those that only analyse similarity of neural activity without applying a decoder. Exclude matches in reviews or citations only. Count different versions of the same study as one item. Ledger counts: slot-filled 5,595; pattern match 33 unique studies; confirmed 23 (all years). At the target's time: 21 prior candidate studies, 8 confirmed (EMG 4, ultrasound or video 2, ECoG 2); [concurrent] 1. Read in full: [[2]](#ref-2), [[9]](#ref-9), [[10]](#ref-10), [[11]](#ref-11).
- M12, neural-speech-reconstruction-identification-test. Inclusion rule: Studies that reconstruct a continuous acoustic speech representation (spectrogram, mel features, envelope or waveform) from neural recordings (EEG, MEG, ECoG, sEEG or intracortical) during perceived, produced, mimed or imagined speech and evaluate the reconstruction. Boundary: classification-only and text-only decoders are excluded; auditory attention decoding that only tracks an envelope to select a talker is counted by the ledger pattern but is not a reconstruction study; versions of one study count once. Design categories are full-text pattern matches and overlap: correlation, identification among candidates, mel-cepstral distortion, human listening test, STOI. Ledger counts: slot-filled 6,960; pattern match 173 unique studies (all years). At the target's time: 7 prior reconstructions of produced, mimed or imagined speech; 3 with identification among candidates or a listener test. Read in full: [[2]](#ref-2), [[3]](#ref-3), [[10]](#ref-10), [[8]](#ref-8). Known-item recall 0.67.
