# Audio-Visual Recognition of Overlapped Speech for the LRS2 Dataset

Yu, J., Zhang, S., Wu, J., Ghorbani, S., Wu, B., Kang, S., Liu, S., Liu, X., Meng, H., Yu, D. · ICASSP 2020 · [doi:10.1109/ICASSP40776.2020.9054127](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP40776.2020.9054127) · [arXiv:2001.01656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01656)

## Verdict

The paper [[1]](#ref-1) changes processing (M06) twice: a gated audio-visual front end inside one LF-MMI recogniser for two-talker mixtures, and a hybrid LF-MMI back end on LRS2; both are standard. Audio-visual fusion weights for a competing talker [[2]](#ref-2) and recognition from the mixture with lip features [[3]](#ref-3), which the authors cite, came first. Gating beats concatenation, 15.20% to 10.31% average WER (Table 3), with no seeds or variance, 24 against 15 TDNN-F layers (Fig. 2, inference) and no clean-test column. The lip-reading state-of-the-art claim fails: Table 1 lists Afouras et al. without their language model (49.8%); with it they report 48.3% [[4]](#ref-4) against 48.86% here. The test-interferer source and mixture count are unstated, unlike three earlier mixture studies (M12, behind); [later] work reaches 6.4% WER at -5 dB on LRS2 [[5]](#ref-5) against 14.22% here.

## Against the field, component by component

### M06 Processing: gated audio-visual fusion in an integrated recogniser

**Usual approach:** Of 89 AVSR decoders public before 2019-07 (pattern match, Appendix), 60 mention feature concatenation and 45 stream weighting; on reading, 4 were tested on mixtures, with concatenation [[3]](#ref-3), stream weights from audio-visual likelihoods [[2]](#ref-2), visual cues in fragment decoding [[6]](#ref-6) and a jointly trained enhancer [[7]](#ref-7). The paper gates AudioNet states (§3.1).

**State of the art:** [Later] (2025) AV-HuBERT fine-tuned on LRS2, VoxCeleb2 and AVYT (1449 h talking-face, 77 h silent-face) gives 6.4% and 2.8% WER at -5 and 10 dB with one interferer on LRS2 test, 2.1% clean, no LM stated [[5]](#ref-5); the best gated system here gives 14.22% and 7.55% with a 4-gram LM (Table 3).

The authors name Chao et al. as, "to the best of our knowledge, the only previous work in this direction" (§1); it is not: Shao and Barker set stream weights from an MLP on audio-visual likelihoods for two-talker mixtures, with a "small" gain over an audio-only estimator [[2]](#ref-2). What is new is learned feature-level gating on overlapped speech: 0 of 89 prior decoders mention gating with overlap (Appendix); gated fusion existed for clean large-vocabulary AVSR [[8]](#ref-8) and, from the same group, for disordered speech [[9]](#ref-9) ([concurrent], cited). At 0 dB concatenation takes the audio-only 52.45% to 16.25% and gating to 10.71% (34% relative, inference); at 10 dB V⊗A (9.05%) is worse than concatenation (8.69%). RecogNet shrinks from 15 to 12 or 9 layers (Fig. 2): the audio path stays at 15 to 18 layers, but the total rises from 15 to 24 (inference), with no parameter count. Integrated against pipelined is 10.31% against 10.92% (5.6% relative, inference); joint training, which the authors defer (§5), existed [[7]](#ref-7) and their [concurrent] follow-up uses it [[10]](#ref-10). Verdict: standard.

### M06 Processing: hybrid LF-MMI back end on LRS2

**Usual approach:** All 6 LRS2 decoders public before 2019-07 mention end-to-end objectives and a language model (pattern match, Appendix), e.g. [[4]](#ref-4) and [[11]](#ref-11). The paper uses a Kaldi TDNN with LF-MMI and a 4-gram LM (§4.1).

**State of the art:** [Later] (2025) Auto-AVSR, trained on LRS2, VoxCeleb2, LRS3 and AVSpeech, scores 1.7% WER on clean LRS2 test as re-evaluated by Nguyen et al., no LM stated [[5]](#ref-5), against 5.93% here (Table 1).

Table 1 takes the no-LM TM-seq2seq row (49.8, 10.5, 9.4); with its 26M-word LM it gives 48.3%, 9.7% and 8.5% (V, A, A+V) [[4]](#ref-4), against an LM of over 2 million words here. So 48.86% lip reading is not ahead, while audio (6.71%) and audio-visual (5.93%) beat those values and 7.0% from hybrid CTC/attention with an RNN-LM [[11]](#ref-11) (15% relative, inference). The authors credit sequence training (CE TDNN 8.95%, §4.2). Verdict: standard.

### M12 Evaluation: simulated two-talker test protocol

**Usual approach:** Of the 89 prior AVSR decoders (pattern match, Appendix), 54 mention stated-SNR tests and 5 run variability; 3 of the 4 tested on mixtures state the interferer source and test size: 2000 male-target, female-masker mixtures [[2]](#ref-2), 600 pairs per TMR in three speaker relations [[6]](#ref-6) and 540 test utterances at one equal-weight SNR [[3]](#ref-3). The paper states test-set references, truncation and four SNRs (§4.1), not the interferer source or mixture count.

**State of the art:** [Later] (2025) clean, one-interferer and two-interferer LRS2 tests in one table [[5]](#ref-5); the [concurrent] same-group study adds 1.2k replayed utterances and finds a gap of around 13% between simulated and replayed data for multi-channel audio-only systems [[10]](#ref-10).

Every test SNR is a training level (§4.1). Verdict: behind on reporting the test mixtures; the rule is met by three earlier read studies, the best being [[6]](#ref-6).

## Where the claims outrun the evidence

- Pipelines are said to need impractical parallel clean targets (§1), yet the integrated system trains on alignments of "the corresponding clean target speech" (§4.1).
- Gating "significantly" outperforms concatenation (§4.4) without seeds and with 9 more layers (Fig. 2, inference).

## What the field needs next

- M06: match parameter count between concatenation and gating, add a clean test, run three seeds.
- M06: train the integrated model without clean-target alignments.
- M12: release the test-mixture lists and add replayed speech, as [concurrent] work began [[10]](#ref-10).

## Questions for the authors

- Which split supplies test interferers, and do test programmes overlap with training?
- How many test mixtures per SNR, and how is AVE averaged?
- What are the parameter counts of the concatenation and gated systems?
- How do the gated systems score on clean test audio?
