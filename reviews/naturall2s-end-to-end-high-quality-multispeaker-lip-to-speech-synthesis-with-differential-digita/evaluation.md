## Strongest confound for the central claim

The central claim is a quality lead with an intelligibility deficit. The strongest confound is that the evaluation conditions differ between systems: author-supplied samples for DiffV2S and the LRS2 Lip2Speech Unit, a pooled 50+50-clip MOS, and cross-paper WER for LipVoicer under a different ASR setup. The review handles this partly: it uses the [5] one-ASR re-score and flags the supplied samples and pooling. The re-score is by the same group, so it is not independent; the review says "same-group", which is enough. The ablation confound (joint training mixed with DDSP removal and a change of vocoder corpus) is handled well.

## Required fixes (ordered by impact)

1. Rewrite the "excludes LipVoicer" bullet in "Where the claims outrun the evidence". On the review's own numbers (DNSMOS 3.23 against 3.11; [5] listener test 3.54 against 2.51), the quality claim holds against LipVoicer too. The weakness is intelligibility (WER 30.4% against 21.4%), not quality.
2. Remove the contradiction about DiffV2S in the M07 Appendix entry. It is counted as "no speaker embedding stated", but the M07 section (quotes c1, c4) says it uses a vision-guided speaker embedding. Reclassify it as video-derived and restate the "none" tally, which leaves the target alone. Then make clear that the M07 "standard" verdict rests on DiffV2S needing no reference audio, not on it using no speaker input.
3. Correct "without statistical tests" (M12). Table III gives ± intervals for every MOS. State that there are intervals on the MOS but no significance tests and no intervals on the objective metrics.
4. Either support "single-run" (M06) and "One run" (M03 row) with a location, or drop them. As written they contradict the question "How many runs underlie Table IV?".
5. Qualify "The DDSP prior is new to video-to-speech". Name the [concurrent] same-group DDSP study, and say that harmonic-plus-noise vocoders from 2008–2015 already appear among the 165 studies (Appendix). The novelty is the differentiable source model trained jointly with the vocoder.
6. Fix the summary table. Rename the M07 row to the use-time speaker slot. Replace the M12 state-of-the-art cell "[5] (2026): re-score" with LipVoicer [6] (2024), WER 21.4%, so that it matches the section text.
7. Give SLD-L2S's own LRS3 row from [5] Table 1, which was read in full. If it beats LipVoicer, it is the current state of the art for M06/M12; if not, say so.
8. In the Verdict, attach the condition to LipVoicer's 21.4%: a cross-paper figure from its own ASR evaluation (LipVoicer Sec. 5.1). Alternatively, cite the [5] one-ASR pair (30.37% against 20.62%) there as M06 does.
9. In the Verdict, say that the authors concede the WER gap to Lip2Speech Unit themselves (§V-B). As written, it reads as the review's own finding.
10. Add claim-table quotes and locations for the statements that have none: "the four compared systems" (Tables I–III), the author-supplied samples (§V), the MOS pooling of LRS2 and LRS3 (Table III), and ground-truth F0 in training with predicted F0 at inference (§IV-D).
11. Make the "first attempt" rebuttal precise. [2] is word-level LRW and [3] is 33 GRID speakers. Say that the claim fails as worded, and that what is new is joint waveform training at sentence level on in-the-wild LRS2/LRS3.

## Scores

- Procedure: 7/10. The claim table is dense and the quotes match the numbers. All arithmetic checks out. The main weaknesses are missing quotes for four statements and one unnamed deciding prior.
- Final text: 6/10. The position is clear and mostly backed, and the voice-similarity reversal and the ablation confound are sharp. However, one "outrun" bullet is contradicted by the review's own numbers, the M07 Appendix tally contradicts the M07 section, and the summary table mislabels a slot and its state of the art.
