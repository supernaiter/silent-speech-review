## Required fixes, ordered by impact

1. **M03 novelty claim is wrong (high).** Chao et al., "Speaker-Targeted Audio-Visual Models for Speech Recognition in Cocktail-Party Environments", Interspeech 2016 (wiki `isca-chao16-spktarget`, before 2019-11, in the AVSR population and in the review's own M12 list of 10 interfering-speech studies) mixes a competing speaker into AVSR training audio. [3] also trains on babble made from 20 LRS2 speech signals. No interfering-speech recall check was run. Remove "new among 107 earlier AVSR studies" and "none mixes interfering speech into training audio" from the Verdict, the M03 section, the slots note, `verdict.json` (short and position) and the row's `expert_take_*`. Name Chao 2016 as the deciding prior (read), and state the actual difference: mixing a single competing utterance with probability 0.1 at 0-20 dB into large-vocabulary end-to-end training, rather than always-on two-speaker GRID mixtures in a DNN-HMM. Run the interfering-speech recall pattern and report it in the Appendix.
2. **M03 count is undercounted (medium-high).** Two read priors add noise in training but are missing from the 7 `noise_in_training` ids: [5] (NOISEX babble 0/5/10 dB, Sec. 5.2) and [6] (multi-condition white noise, Sec. 3.3). Chao 2016 also trains on corrupted audio. "100 of 107 name neither" is therefore at most 97. Correct the count or call it a lower bound. Mention the known-item recall of 0.5 next to it.
3. **M06 table count is mentions, not designs (medium-high).** "HMM decoding; 79 of 123" counts any study that names an HMM. All four typical works the review cites are in that list and none uses HMM decoding. Under the brief, the component-table count must be a design count at 2019-11. Either reclassify by the objective used, or relabel the cell as mentions (upper bound) and do not call HMM decoding the usual approach for sentence-level lip reading and AVSR in 2019.
4. **M02 wording and the [3] corpus (medium).** Replace "the exception, [4]" with the Appendix basis ("5 hits, the only single video corpus of 1,000 h or more is [4]"). Say that [3] trained on about 1.4k h combined from three corpora (its Table 1; target Table 7), because the review itself compares 31k with 1.4k h. Check the LipNet hit (1611.01599), which looks spurious.
5. **M12 usual approach (medium).** 50 of 107 is not a majority: 51 of 107 do not test additive noise at stated SNRs. Either state "the largest group, 50 of 107" or name the majority practice. For the M12 state of the art, use [7]'s 0 dB babble noisy result (Table 3) rather than the clean 0.76%.
6. **Credit the authors' own claims (medium, fairness).** (a) Variable-rate sync: the authors say "in contrast to previous AV-ASR work" (Sec. 1). Write "as the authors state, and none of the 107 does (Appendix)". (b) The small clean A/V gain: the authors say "slightly improves" (Sec. 6) and "about a 5% relative improvement" (Sec. 4.4). (c) Pan and tilt: the authors give this as their belief about why they get more data than V2P (Sec. 2), not as the reason for the 150k -> 31k yield.
7. **M07 cell (low-medium).** The cell reads "Not stated" with verdict not reported, so there is no filled-cell contradiction. But part of the slot is reported: character outputs give "a simple way of achieving an open-vocabulary system" (Sec. 1, so no lexicon), and decoding is a beam of 4 (Sec. 4). Narrow the cell to "External LM not stated; lexicon-free characters" and keep not reported for the external LM only.
8. **Comparator confound in Table 7 (low-medium).** The TM-seq2seq LRS3 numbers from [3] come from decoding with an external LM (afouras2018 Sec. 3.6), and V2P uses FST decoding. The RNN-T apparently uses none. [2] lists TM-seq2seq training at 0.5K h, not 1.4k h. Add this to the LRS3 bullet or to the LM question. Optionally note the target's citation slip ([7] for TM-seq2seq, Sec. 2).
9. **Missed caveat (low).** Sec. 4.3: under a different learning-rate schedule, fixed 30 fps was slightly better than variable. Add it to the M06 "no gain, no cost" sentence.
10. **State-of-the-art details (low).** 0.9% LRS3 AVSR was first reached by the VGG Conformer (Chang et al., TMLR 2022; [2] Table 2), and [2] trains with 50% video dropout. Say so in M03, where the state of the art is a modality-dropout system. The M06 table cell gives [2], while the text calls 0.76% [7] the best value. Make them agree, or label [2] as the RNN-T lineage. A lower LRS3 AVSR WER after 2024 is possible: unverified.
11. **Presentation (low).** Duplicate "[later] [later]" labels on refs 2, 7 and 8. Ref 2 is dated 2024 (ICASSP), but the text says "In 2023": add "first public 2023-02". Insufficient quotes for claim-table rows 22 (CI sentence) and 28 (V 48.5).

No `ahead` or `behind` verdicts are given. The three `standard` verdicts for M02, M03 and M06 follow the rules ("difference not supported well enough to call": one run, one non-public noisy test set, no decoder ablation). The verdicts judge the protocol, not the results. M06's absence claim is backed by read priors and a recall check. M03's is not (fix 1). Chronology labels are correct, and no later work is treated as prior work.
