## Judgement on the M12 "behind" verdict

- Rule check: "at least 3 earlier ledger papers meet a condition the target does not, with the best of them read and named". All three ([2], [3], [4]) are in the ledger, are sentence-level AVSR under additive noise, date from before 2022-01, and were read. The threshold is met only under the broad condition "any variability estimate (test-set CI or repeated runs)", which is the Appendix wording ("run or test-set variability"). Under the condition named in the M12 heading, usual-approach line and table ("run variability", "run-to-run variability"), only [3] fully qualifies. [4] repeated one auxiliary experiment and explicitly declined significance testing for its main systems, and [2] gives test-set CIs only. As written, the review uses two different conditions. Also, the text never names which of the three is "the best"; [2] is called the deciding prior only in the References.
- Comparability: same task, different setting. [2] trains on a proprietary 31k-hour set and reports CIs only on YTDEV18. [3] and [4] use small LSTM or Transformer models on TCD-TIMIT or LRS2, with CER and cafeteria noise. None is a self-supervised, LRS3, 600K-step and 64-GPU setting, where five seeds of pre-training are a real cost. A test-set CI as in [2] costs nothing, so the fair yardstick is [2]'s, not [3]'s.
- Majority practice: by the review's own tallies, run variability appears in 33 of 349 studies (all dates). Before 2022-01, the read set is three studies out of 143. The target therefore matches the field's usual practice, and the `standard` rule ("matches the usual approach from the ledger tally") also applies. SKILL.md allows `behind` against "best comparable work", so this is not a rule violation. But it is fair only if the text (a) uses one condition throughout ("a variability estimate: test-set CI or repeated runs"), (b) names [2] as the best comparable work and says that a test-set CI was available at no extra cost, (c) states plainly that most of the field, including [10], also omits variability, and (d) corrects the usual-approach count (row 14). Otherwise downgrade to `standard` and keep the omission as a limitation and a question.
- Evaluator position: `behind` holds by the letter of the rule but rests on a mislabelled usual-approach count and an inconsistent condition. As written, it is not adequately supported.

## Judgement on M03 "standard"

- Novelty and absence: the tally supports "none of the 143 pre-2022 AVSR studies" (141 without self-supervised pre-training; the 2 matches are citations, read; 0 with noise in pre-training). The evaluator knows of no earlier AVSR study that adds noise during self-supervised pre-training. Self-supervised training with noise contamination existed earlier for audio-only speech (PASE+, Ravanelli et al., ICASSP 2020, arXiv:2001.09239). Concurrent audio-only work is WavLM (arXiv:2110.13900, 2021-10), which the authors cite; it is [concurrent], not prior. So "new for AVSR" is correct but is a transfer, and the authors say as much. Known-item recall of 0.5 should temper the wording ("pattern match" is stated, which is enough).
- Repetition reasoning: `standard` follows the rule (one test set, no seeds). However, the stated reason ("the new part ... buys 1.5 and 1.1 points") and "one run per cell" go beyond the evidence (rows 3, 13), and Table 4 is ignored.

## Required fixes, by impact

1. M12 usual approach (text and table): 183 is the additive-noise pattern count, not "additive noise with no run variability". The audit's example pids include studies that report variability (arxiv-1911.04890, arxiv-2602.19316). Restate as "183 of 349 test with additive noise at stated SNRs; 33 of 349 report run or test-set variability (pattern match, all dates)". Give the pre-2022 counts so that the time base matches M03.
2. M12 verdict: use one condition throughout. Either keep `behind` with the condition "a variability estimate (test-set CI or repeated runs)", name [2] as the best comparable work, describe [2] as test-set CIs and [4] as one auxiliary experiment, and say that most of the field also omits variability. Or downgrade to `standard`.
3. "one run on one test set" and "one run per cell": the paper is silent on run count. Write "no run count, seed or variability is reported (Sec. 3.1; released configs fix seed 1337)". This also resolves the clash with Question 1.
4. Speech-noise speaker overlap: report the authors' statement "we ensured there are no speaker overlap among different partitions" (Sec. 3.1), reframe the bullet as a paper-code discrepancy, and revise Question 3.
5. Misquote: replace "fewer labeled hours" with the source wording "fewer labeled data".
6. M12 state of the art: give the label condition for 0.76% (1,759 h labelled, trained without noise). Add Whisper-Flamingo's noisy AVSR 5.6% against 5.8%. Drop "same noise file" or support it. Consider citing [9]'s three-seed mean ± std as the protocol state of the art.
7. M03 attribution: the target cites [5] ("following [15]") for the 0 dB, p=0.25 recipe, and the recipe matches [6]. Say both.
8. M03 reasoning: mention Table 4 (BASE: 28.2 to 12.5, 13.1 to 10.3). Base `standard` on the missing seeds and the single test set for the whole M03 change, including self-supervised pre-training, not on the size of the noise-pre-training gain.
9. Fairness: say that the authors themselves claim the novelty (Sec. 2.2, Sec. 4) and cite concurrent WavLM as similar.
10. M03 state of the art, NOISEX row: add "LRS3-only unlabelled" to the Base condition.
11. Add "(inference)" to the 1.5 and 1.1 point differences in the Verdict.
12. Add the target's "not been studied in prior work" overclaim against [2]'s 2019 overlapping-speech test to "Where the claims outrun the evidence".
13. 13-components table: show denominators ("141 of 143, pattern match"; "183 of 349, pattern match"). The M12 cell must describe what 183 counts (fix 1).

Unverified outside knowledge (no fix required): later LRS3 AVSR results below 0.76% may exist, for example MMS-LLaMA (ACL 2025), which the evaluator recalls at about 0.7%. This could not be located here.

## Scores

- Procedure: 6/10. All sources needed were read, and the quotes are mostly exact. But the deciding-prior conditions were not checked against the verdict's condition (test-set CI vs run variability; [4]'s scope), the usual-approach design label was not checked against its pids, and an explicit author statement (speaker-disjoint noise partitions) was missed.
- Final text: 6/10. The text has a clear position and the confound analysis of the 49.6% claim is sound. But the M12 usual-approach count is mislabelled, the behind verdict rests on an inconsistent condition, one quotation is not in the source, and there is one unsupported "one run" assertion.

Strengths (brief): the review separates recipe from pre-training effect in the headline comparison, correctly flags the noise-source asymmetry against Xu et al., uses the released code to find the seed and LM weight, and labels chronology correctly.
