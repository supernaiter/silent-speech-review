## Full review

### In short

Key mid-period survey that widens SSI to all speech biosignals; reviewed here from the abstract only.

### Review

Written by many of the field's core groups, this survey reframes silent speech as part of a broader area of biosignal-based spoken communication, covering signals from the articulators, the muscles that move them, the neural pathways and the brain. The abstract positions the goals as everyday communication devices, speech rehabilitation and a better understanding of speech production, and emphasizes the interdisciplinary nature of the work. Only the abstract and bibliographic metadata could be accessed for this review, so specific modality comparisons, numeric results and conclusions are not summarized here and should be checked against the full text.

### Why it matters

A bridge between the 2010 SSI definition and later neural speech-decoding work, placing articulatory, muscular and neural signals in one framework.

### Prior state of the art

SSI was framed mainly around articulatory and muscle sensing, while neural speech decoding developed largely in parallel in the BCI community.

### What changed

Treats articulatory, muscular and neural speech biosignals as one research area with shared goals in communication, rehabilitation and speech science.

### Position in the field

Widely cited survey linking SSI and speech BCI; companion to the TASLP special issue on biosignal-based speech processing.

### Practical value

Useful entry point for readers moving between EMG/articulatory SSI and neural speech decoding.
